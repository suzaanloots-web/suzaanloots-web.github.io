The Story Behind the Chocolate

From a pink birthday cake at age four to couverture sculpted entirely by hand — this is how it started, and why it never stopped.

“Dit is in my wese: ek droom oor koek en obsesseer oor mooi koekgoed. Dis my manier om kuns tot 'n mate uit te beeld.”
It is in my nature: I dream about cake and obsess over beautiful bakes — my way of expressing art. · Kanya Hunt

I was four years old when my mother first let me help in the kitchen. I still remember that first cake — pink, with pink icing.

Two decades later, I work exclusively with fine couverture chocolate — sculpting every decorative element entirely by hand. The protea petals. The delicate ruffles. The spirals. Everything you see is chocolate, and everything is made by me.

My background in dietetics gives me a rare analytical understanding of what makes flavour work — how layers interact, how richness balances, how texture and temperature create a complete experience. I don't just bake. I design something you taste long after you've eaten it.

✦ “In my spare time I run and cycle — because then I can eat more cake.”

A Different Kind of Cake

Cape Town's wedding cake market is dominated by fondant — flawless white tiers built for the photograph. Kanya Hunt works in an entirely different medium, producing cakes that are visually extraordinary and genuinely extraordinary to eat.

“Not just beautiful to look at — made to be the best thing anyone eats that day.”

Chocolate is not Kanya's specialty — it is her language. Where other artists reach for fondant, she reaches for fine couverture, sculpting it into ruffles, proteas, spirals, and roses that look almost too beautiful to cut. Almost. Because these cakes are designed as dessert — deep, indulgent, layered — giving your guests something to remember long after the evening is over.
